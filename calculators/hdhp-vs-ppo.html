How this calculator works

A plan's real cost is premiums plus what you pay when you use care. The HSA plan usually has lower premiums and a higher deductible, and it comes with two kinds of help: your employer's HSA deposit, and the tax you skip on your own deposit.

  • Out-of-pocket cost = your costs up to the deductible, then your coinsurance share, capped at the out-of-pocket maximum.
  • Your own HSA deposit is what it takes to cover your out-of-pocket costs after the employer deposit, up to the family limit. It saves federal income tax plus Social Security and Medicare tax, because it goes through payroll.
  • The bad-year check assumes both plans hit their out-of-pocket maximum and you deposit enough to cover it, up to the limit.

Defaults are 2026 US averages for family coverage (KFF) and a typical family-of-four spending year (MEPS, total care costs paid by all payers: 1 in 4 families of four spend under $4,883, half under $11,790, 1 in 10 over $50,849, in 2024 dollars). The out-of-pocket maximums ($8,000 / $10,000) are example values, because KFF does not report an average family out-of-pocket maximum: enter yours. Not modeled: copays, separate drug deductibles, out-of-network bills, state income tax. California and New Jersey don't follow the federal HSA tax break, so HSA contributions are taxed by those states (California FTB 3805P instructions; New Jersey Assembly Bill A2634 statement, 2026).
