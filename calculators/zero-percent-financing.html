0% financing vs deferred interest calculator

"No interest if paid in full" is not the same as a 0% APR. If even a little is left at the deadline, a deferred interest deal can charge interest on the whole purchase, back to the day you bought it. Enter the price, the promo length, the card's rate and what you plan to pay each month.

How this calculator works

To owe no interest, you need to pay the price divided by the number of promo months, every month, on time. If you pay less, something is left at the deadline. On a deferred interest deal, the bill then adds the interest that was counted all along at the card's full rate. On a true 0% intro APR card, interest only starts on what's left, from the deadline on.

The back interest uses the same simplified monthly method as the CFPB's own worked example: each month's balance after that month's payment, times the APR divided by 12. Card issuers use daily balances, so a real charge is usually a bit higher. Our version reproduces the CFPB example exactly: $4,500 over 24 months with $180 left at 31.99% gives $1,439.55.

Assumptions: one purchase on the card, no other balances, every payment on time, no fees. With other purchases on the same card, extra payments go to the highest-rate balance first, except in the last two billing cycles before the deadline (Regulation Z, 12 CFR 1026.53(b)). Being more than 60 days late can end the promo early. This is for credit cards only. Buy now, pay later plans and car loans have their own terms.
