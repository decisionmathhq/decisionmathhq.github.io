How this calculator works

Traditional (pre-tax) 401(k) money skips federal income tax, so raising your rate for the last paychecks lowers this year's tax. It does not skip Social Security and Medicare tax, so the tax saved comes from your federal bracket only. Each paycheck shrinks by the extra you put in minus the tax you save.

  • The limit applies across all your 401(k) plans for the year (IRS 401(k) contribution limits page). Age 50 or older can add $8,000 for 2026; ages 60 to 63 can add $11,250 instead.
  • If last year's Social Security wages from your employer topped $150,000, catch-up money must go in as Roth (2026).
  • A "true-up" pays the full match at year-end. Check your plan documents or ask HR.
  • Roth 401(k) contributions save no tax now and are not modeled here.

Assumptions: federal tax only, standard deduction, W-2 wages, no bonuses or other pre-tax deductions, actual paycheck withholding not modeled. The match default (50% up to 6%) is an example, not an average: about half of workers in such plans had a 6% cap (BLS, 2015 data). Enter your own plan.

Sources

  • 2026 401(k) deferral limit ($24,500), catch-up ($8,000; $11,250 at 60 to 63), Roth catch-up wage threshold ($150,000): IRS IR-2025-111; final regulations T.D. 10033.
  • 401(k) deferrals still count as wages for Social Security and Medicare: IRS Topic 424.
  • 2026 tax brackets and standard deduction: IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32.
  • Limit applies across all plans: IRS, 401(k) and profit-sharing plan contribution limits.
  • Match caps: BLS Beyond the Numbers, Vol. 5 No. 17 (2015 data).
